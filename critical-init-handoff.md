# Handoff: Pachitariu & Stringer 2026, "A critical initialization for biological neural networks"

Context for a Claude Code session. Goal is to reproduce the paper's core spectral
claims numerically and stress-test two points where the argument may be fragile.

## The model

Linear dynamics with independent stochastic input:

    tau * dx/dt = -x + A x + eps,   eps ~ N(0, I)

Drift matrix `M = (A - I)/tau`. A is a random matrix, mean-subtracted (representing
global inhibitory stabilization), then divided by a scalar so its spectral radius is
R (the paper uses R = 0.998, "critically normalized"). Diagonal set to zero.

Stationary covariance solves the Lyapunov equation `M Sigma + Sigma M^T + Q = 0`
with Q = I. This holds for **any stable A**. Only when A is symmetric does it admit
the closed form

    Sigma = 0.5 * inv(I - A)

because then Sigma and M commute. Consequences of symmetry:
- Sigma and A share eigenvectors
- `lambda_Sigma = 1 / (2 * (1 - lambda_A))`
- the system separates into N independent scalar OU processes in the eigenbasis

## The 2/3 derivation

1. Wigner semicircle: eigenvalues of a symmetric random matrix have density
   `p(lambda) ~ sqrt(R^2 - lambda^2)`. Universal in the entry distribution.
2. Rank as area. With `lambda = R cos(theta)`, the rank of an eigenvalue is a
   circular segment area, `n = 0.5 * (theta - cos(theta) sin(theta))`. Near the edge
   the leading terms cancel and `n ~ theta^3 / 3`. The square-root vanishing of the
   density at the edge is what produces the cube.
3. Lyapunov map. At R = 1: `1 - lambda_A ~ theta^2 / 2`, so `lambda_Sigma ~ theta^-2`.
4. Compose: `lambda_Sigma ~ n^(-2/3)`.

Generalization worth checking numerically: if the edge density behaved as
`(1 - lambda)^beta`, the exponent would be `1/(beta + 1)`. Semicircle has beta = 1/2.

Non-symmetric A gives ~1.25 empirically, with no closed form. Note that the circular
law's projection onto the real axis IS a semicircle, so the 2/3 vs 1.25 gap is
attributable to eigenvector **non-normality**, not to complex eigenvalues per se.

## Effect of spectral radius (Fig. 2i)

Scaling A is NOT equivalent to rescaling tau, because the leak term `-I` is not
scaled with it. With gap `Delta = 1 - R`:

    lambda_Sigma ~ 1 / (2*Delta + R*theta^2)

so the spectrum is flat for `theta^2 << 2*Delta/R` and crosses over to the -2/3 power
law above it. Knee at `n* ~ Delta^(3/2)`. The 2/3 asymptote is never destroyed; it is
pushed to higher rank. The paper fits ranks 10-500, so a knee inside that window drags
the fitted slope down. This is why reported alpha falls from ~0.70 at R = 0.998 to
~0.48 at R = 0.975.

## Experiments to run

1. **Semicircle and the rank relation.** Sample symmetric A (N ~ 4000-10000), confirm
   the eigenvalue density, and check `n ~ theta^3/3` empirically near the edge.

2. **Closed-form vs numerical Lyapunov.** Compute Sigma both via `0.5*inv(I-A)` and via
   `scipy.linalg.solve_lyapunov`. Confirm they agree for symmetric A and diverge for
   non-symmetric A. Then check how far the eigenvectors of Sigma rotate away from those
   of A as asymmetry increases.

3. **Exponent vs spectral radius.** Sweep R over roughly 0.95 to 0.999. For each,
   (a) fit alpha over ranks 10-500 as the paper does, and (b) fit only above the
   predicted knee `n* ~ Delta^(3/2)`. Prediction: (a) reproduces the rising curve in
   Fig. 2i, (b) stays near 2/3 throughout. If (b) holds, Fig. 2i is a windowing
   artifact rather than a change in the underlying law, which is the substantive claim
   to verify.

4. **Universality.** Repeat with Bernoulli, Gaussian, truncated Gaussian, exponential
   entries. Expect alpha ~ 0.69 in all cases (their Extended Data Fig. 1).

5. **Degrees of symmetry.** Build `A = s*S + (1-s)*G` with S symmetric and G Ginibre,
   sweep s, and track both the fitted alpha and a non-normality measure such as
   `||A^T A - A A^T||_F`. Question: does alpha track symmetry or non-normality?

Simulating the SDE is not required for 1-5; the stationary covariance is available in
closed form or from the Lyapunov solver. Euler-Maruyama integration is only needed to
reproduce rastermaps and timescales (paper uses dt = 2 ms, tau = 20 ms, N = 10000).

## Open questions carried over

- The SVCA2 estimator is calibrated on simulations generated by the same linear model
  whose exponent the paper then confirms in data. Extended Data Fig. 4c sweeps
  ground-truth exponents ~0.6 to 1.4, which may or may not defuse the circularity.
- alpha is jointly sensitive to symmetry and to distance from criticality, and the two
  push in opposite directions. A partially non-symmetric matrix at criticality and a
  symmetric matrix below it can both land near 0.8. The paper separates them via DMD
  rotational content (Fig. 3), not via the exponent.
